Baseball History

The Evolution of America's Pastime

By Ryan Brady

⚾

Historic Baseball Photograph

The Origins of Baseball

Baseball has been a part of American culture for nearly two centuries. While its exact origins are debated, the game developed from earlier bat-and-ball games and began taking its modern form during the mid-1800s.

In 1869, the Cincinnati Red Stockings became the first openly all-professional baseball team. The National League was established in 1876, followed by the American League in 1901. The first modern World Series took place in 1903, laying the foundation for Major League Baseball as we know it today.

The Growth of the Game

Throughout the 20th century, baseball became known as America's pastime. Players such as Babe Ruth, Lou Gehrig, Willie Mays, and Hank Aaron helped shape the identity of the sport and inspired generations of baseball players.

One of the most significant moments in baseball history came in 1947, when Jackie Robinson broke Major League Baseball's modern-era color barrier. His courage and accomplishments helped change not only baseball but American society as a whole.

Over the years, legendary performances, historic rivalries, and unforgettable World Series moments have helped make baseball one of the most celebrated sports in the country.

The Evolution of Baseball

Although the fundamentals of baseball have remained relatively consistent, the way the game is played has changed significantly.

Advancements in technology, strength training, nutrition, analytics, and player development have transformed how players prepare and compete. Pitchers are throwing harder, teams are making more data-driven decisions, and players have access to training resources that previous generations could only imagine.

Major League Baseball has also introduced new rules, such as the pitch clock, to improve the pace of play and enhance the fan experience.

⚾

Modern Baseball Stadium Photograph

A Global Game

Although baseball is traditionally associated with the United States, it has grown into an international sport. Countries such as Japan, the Dominican Republic, Venezuela, South Korea, and Cuba have produced some of the most talented players in the world.

Events like the World Baseball Classic demonstrate how deeply the game is embedded in cultures around the globe.

A Timeless Sport

One of the greatest things about baseball is its ability to evolve while maintaining its traditions. Despite changes in technology, rules, and playing styles, the basic objective of the game remains the same.

From neighborhood baseball fields to Major League stadiums, the game continues to bring people together, create lasting memories, and inspire the next generation of players.

Baseball Today

Record Contracts and an Uncertain Future

By Ryan Brady

⚾

Major League Baseball Photograph

Record-Breaking Contracts

Major League Baseball has seen some of the largest contracts in sports history in recent years. A few years ago, Shohei Ohtani signed a 10-year, $700 million contract with the Los Angeles Dodgers, while Juan Soto signed a 15-year, $765 million contract with the New York Mets. Just this past week, Tarik Skubal signed a record breaking contract for a pitcher with the New York Mets for $450 million over 9 years

While these contracts show the financial strength of baseball, they have also brought attention to the growing payroll differences between teams. Some organizations can afford to spend hundreds of millions of dollars on free agents, while others rely heavily on drafting and developing their own players.

The Potential MLB Lockout

With MLB's collective bargaining agreement set to expire in December 2026, there is growing concern about a potential lockout.

One of the biggest topics surrounding the negotiations is the implementation of a salary cap. Unlike other major professional sports leagues, MLB does not have a traditional salary cap.

Owners who support a salary cap believe it would create a more competitive league by reducing the financial gap between teams. Players, however, are strongly opposed to a salary cap because it would limit how much teams can spend on player salaries.

⚾

Major League Stadium Photograph

What's Next for Baseball?

Baseball is in an interesting position. Players are receiving record-breaking contracts, but disagreements between owners and players could put the 2027 season at risk.

It will be interesting to see whether both sides can reach an agreement that avoids a lockout and allows baseball to continue without interruption.

Player Development

The Road to the Big Leagues

By Ryan Brady

⚾

Youth Baseball Photograph

Becoming a professional baseball player requires years of hard work, dedication, and development. While every player's journey is different, there are several common steps along the road to Major League Baseball.

1. Youth Baseball

Youth baseball is all about learning the fundamentals and developing a love for the game. Players should focus on hitting, throwing, fielding, and understanding how baseball is played. Most importantly, they should enjoy playing.

2. High School Baseball

High school is when baseball typically becomes more competitive. Players begin focusing more on their physical development, mechanics, strength training, and overall performance.

For those hoping to play at the next level, high school is an important time to gain exposure to college coaches and professional scouts. Some players are drafted directly out of high school, while others choose to continue their development in college.

3. College Baseball (Optional)

College baseball is not a requirement for becoming a professional baseball player. However, it provides players with an opportunity to develop their skills while competing at a higher level.

Players gain access to more advanced coaching, training facilities, and development resources. College players may enter professional baseball through the MLB Draft or by signing as an undrafted free agent.

⚾

Professional Baseball Photograph

4. Minor League Baseball

Signing a professional contract is only the beginning. Most players spend time developing throughout the minor league system, which includes:

  1. Rookie Ball
    • Introduction to professional baseball
  2. Single-A
    • Developing fundamental skills
  3. High-A
    • Facing more advanced competition
  4. Double-A
    • Refining skills against advanced prospects
  5. Triple-A
    • Preparing for Major League Baseball
    • Some players currently on major league rosters but not activated

As players advance through each level, the competition becomes increasingly challenging. Consistency, preparation, recovery, and mental toughness become extremely important.

5. Major League Baseball

Reaching the major leagues is the ultimate goal for many baseball players. It requires a combination of talent, hard work, opportunity, and perseverance.

There is no single path to the big leagues. From my own experience, one of the most important things a player can do is focus on improving each day rather than constantly worrying about reaching the next level.

Stay Motivated

The road to the big leagues is not easy. Stay committed to your goals, embrace the challenges, and never stop working to improve.

About Me & My Career

My Journey Through Baseball

By Ryan Brady

⚾

Ryan Brady Pitching Photograph

Where It All Began

I started playing baseball when I was six years old. Growing up, all of my friends played, so naturally, I got involved too. At first, baseball was simply something I enjoyed doing with my friends.

It wasn't until high school, when baseball became much more competitive, that I truly fell in love with the game. I enjoyed the competition and the challenges that came with playing at a higher level. It was also during high school that I began to realize I had a legitimate opportunity to play professional baseball.

From College to Professional Baseball

After high school, I continued my baseball career at Brigham Young University (BYU). After three seasons at BYU, I was offered a minor league contract by the Milwaukee Brewers in 2022. What had started as a childhood hobby had turned into a professional career.

Adjusting to Professional Baseball

One of the biggest adjustments I faced entering professional baseball was the length of the season. Playing a schedule of approximately 140 games, combined with weekly travel, was significantly different from college baseball. Learning how to manage the physical and mental demands of a long season became an important part of my development.

Another major adjustment was transitioning from a starting pitcher to a relief pitcher. Rather than preparing for a scheduled start each week, I had to adjust my routine and learn how to be ready to pitch on any given day.

A New Opportunity in Kansas City

During my time with the Milwaukee Brewers organization, I worked my way up to the Double-A level. In December 2023, I was traded to the Kansas City Royals organization, beginning a new chapter in my professional career.

I continued my development as a relief pitcher with the Royals, eventually reaching Triple-A, the highest level of minor league baseball.

Reaching Triple-A and performing well at that level is what I consider the greatest accomplishment of my professional career so far. Unfortunately, a shoulder injury interrupted my progress and forced me to step away from the game.

⚾

Ryan Brady Professional Career Photograph

The Next Chapter

Although my injury has presented a significant challenge, I don't consider my baseball journey finished. My current focus is on recovering, rebuilding my strength, and giving myself an opportunity to return to professional baseball.

Through BASEBALL, I hope to share my knowledge of the game, my experiences as a professional baseball player, and a perspective on baseball that goes beyond what fans typically see on the field.

There is still more to my story, and I'm looking forward to seeing where baseball takes me next.
